01 · Workflow fit

Where can voice AI create value?

Start with a repeated conversation that has a clear purpose, usable information and an observable next step. The opportunity is strongest when a slow response has a commercial or operational cost.

Define the customer need

For travel, that might be exploring flight options or resolving a booking question. For a service business, it could be qualifying an enquiry and finding a suitable appointment.

Map the next action

Identify which records, availability or supplier information the solution needs, and what it should do when those systems are unavailable.

Set the human boundary

Decide which decisions require a person and how that person receives the customer's context.

Choose a useful measure

Track completed next steps, qualified enquiries, resolution or handling time alongside quality and escalation. Call volume alone does not show whether a solution is helping.

02 · Rollout readiness

What should a first rollout prove?

A focused pilot should answer a business question: can this workflow meet the quality, operational and commercial measures your team agreed?

  1. Establish a baseline. Understand today's demand, response times, completion rates and recurring failure points.
  2. Agree scope and ownership. Name the workflow owner, required integrations, access permissions and escalation path.
  3. Test realistic exceptions. Include corrections, interruptions, unclear requests, unavailable appointments and system failures.
  4. Review outcomes and quality together. Examine whether the right action happened, whether the customer understood it, and whether a person had to recover the interaction.
  5. Make a rollout decision. Expand, adjust or pause based on the evidence and the team's capacity to supervise the service.

03 · Partner fit

What makes a white-label offer workable?

A clear market need is only the beginning. A repeatable offer also needs agreed delivery responsibilities and a support model clients can rely on.

Client relationship

Agree who handles discovery, commercial conversations, onboarding and ongoing communication.

Delivery and support

Define what is configurable, what needs a new build, who responds to issues and how changes are approved.

Brand and experience

Align the voice, terminology and hand-offs with the partner's proposition and customer expectations.

Repeatable economics

Consider implementation effort, usage, supervision and support alongside the commercial offer.
