AI sales guide

A short guide to
your first AI agent.

Two things to work out before you spend anything: where you’re losing sales, and what a first pilot needs to prove.

Where you’re losing salesWhat a pilot should prove
01 · Where you’re losing sales

Find the moment that costs you most.

Pick one moment that happens often and costs you work: the call you can’t take on a job, the enquiry that lands on a Sunday night, the quote nobody chased. Then try to answer the four questions below. If you can’t yet, you’re not ready to build anything, and that’s useful to know.

What does the customer want?

Be specific. “Book a boiler repair this week” is something an agent can do; “get some help” isn’t. Start with a request you hear again and again.

What should happen next?

A booking, a callback, a quote chased, a question answered. List the information each one needs (prices, availability, job status) and where it lives today. If it only lives in your head, start there.

When should a person take over?

Complaints, large jobs, anything urgent or unsafe. Decide in advance, and make sure whoever takes over gets the full story so the customer doesn’t have to repeat it.

How will you know it worked?

Choose a measure tied to sales, such as appointments booked or quotes followed up, and note today’s figure before you start. Call volume on its own tells you very little.

02 · Run a focused pilot

What should a focused pilot prove?

One question, really: does the agent help your customers and your business in the way you agreed? Five checks get you to an answer.

  1. Write down today’s numbers. How many enquiries come in, how fast they get a reply, how many become bookings or sales, and where they tend to go wrong.
  2. Agree the agent’s job. What it handles, which tools it connects to, what it can see and who takes over when a person is needed.
  3. Test the awkward calls. Changes of mind, interruptions, unclear requests, fully booked days, a system that’s down.
  4. Listen back. Did the right thing happen? Did the customer understand what was agreed? Did someone have to step in and put it right?
  5. Decide: expand, adjust or stop. Base it on the results, and on how much time you have to keep an eye on the agent.
